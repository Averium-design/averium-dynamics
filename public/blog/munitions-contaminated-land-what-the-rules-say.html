Munitions-contaminated land: the number everyone quotes is not what the directive says

12 November 2026 · 6 min read · Averium Dynamics UG, Berlin Auf Deutsch lesen

Anyone who looks into wildfires on munitions-contaminated land soon comes across a number. Sometimes it is 500 metres, sometimes 1,000, sometimes 300. It is almost always attributed to FwDV 500.

FwDV 500 is the fire service directive for CBRN incidents (Dienstvorschrift für den ABC-Einsatz). There is no separate directive for unexploded ordnance (Kampfmittel). The federal states' guidance documents (Handlungsempfehlungen) fall back on it for that reason, and the numbers drift along the way.

This is not an academic problem. Anyone who puts a number in a memo, a tender or a quote should be able to say where it comes from.

What it actually says

The basic values in FwDV 500 are a danger zone (Gefahrenbereich) of about 50 metres and a cordon (Absperrbereich) of about 100 metres. Where there is a risk of explosion or of a container bursting (Zerknallgefahr), the danger zone has to be extended considerably.

For larger quantities of explosives or military munitions exposed to fire, the AFKzV's explanatory notes to FwDV 500 give a distance of at least 300 metres with adequate cover, plus a cordon of 1,000 metres. The Hessian guidance on forest and vegetation fires in munitions-contaminated areas gives the same values, word for word: the distance from the hazard must be at least 300 metres with adequate cover (“Der Abstand vom Gefahrenobjekt muss bei ausreichender Deckung mindestens 300 Meter betragen”).

Two parts of that sentence regularly get lost when it is quoted.

The first is with adequate cover (bei ausreichender Deckung). Without cover, the value does not apply.

The second is that the distance can be adjusted after further reconnaissance and assessment of the hazards. It is not a fixed limit. It is a decision for the incident command, based on what it knows about the ground.

Alongside this, the professional discussion works with 500 metres. The May 2026 working draft of the training and tactics sub-group (UAG Ausbildung und Taktik) of the federal-state working group on national wildfire protection (BLoAG Nationaler Waldbrandschutz) gives 500 metres with adequate cover, and next to it a value of 1,000 metres according to the DGUV, the German statutory accident insurance.

So the literature is not consistent on this point. That is the honest position, and it is more useful than a tidy number that does not survive a follow-up question.

The distinction that decides the tactics

What kind of ground it is matters more than the number. The Hessian guidance separates the two cleanly.

That is the difference between ground where nothing can be done and ground where work is allowed as long as nobody finds anything.

In Brandenburg, around 575,000 hectares are classed as suspected of containing unexploded ordnance (kampfmittelverdächtig), about a fifth of the state's area, of which around 300,000 hectares are forest. Suspected, not confirmed. Most of it therefore falls into the second row of the table.

Anyone who lumps the two together describes the situation as stricter than it is.

What is done on this ground today

  • Holding lines and firebreaks (Riegelstellungen, Schutzstreifen), plus cleared corridors, tracks and bare sandy ground where the fire is held
  • Attacking the fire from cover, with water cannons and unmanned monitors that are set down at the control line and operated from there
  • Controlled burn-out of the interior, which on confirmed contaminated ground is the correct decision and the one the rules prescribe
  • Air support from helicopters and air tankers, which has to be requested and depends on the weather
  • Reconnaissance by drone
  • Remote-controlled firefighting tracked vehicles and clearing equipment
  • Involving the explosive ordnance disposal service (Kampfmittelbeseitigungsdienst), which decides what is possible on the ground, and the forestry administration through its forest fire protection officer (Waldbrandschutzbeauftragter)
  • Prevention: converting the forest to more resistant stands (Waldumbau), firebreak systems, tracks, firefighting water wells, clearing individual corridors

These methods work. They work at the edge of the area, from cover, or after a delay.

Unmanned systems for suppression

For reconnaissance, drones have long been established on this kind of ground. For suppression, they are being tested internationally.

According to Chinese state media, firefighting drones were used to fight the Sichuan wildfire in March 2024, not only for reconnaissance. The source is state media, and that needs saying.

Korean Air and Anduril Industries have been developing an AI-supported wildfire suppression platform for South Korea together since October 2025. According to the companies involved, the software handles detection and command, and suppression is carried out by rapidly available unmanned aircraft or by crewed air tankers.

For munitions-contaminated ground, the deciding point is not the drop. It is whether an aircraft works reliably without satellite navigation and without a secured view of the ground. On this ground, neither a survey beforehand nor a recovery afterwards is possible.
